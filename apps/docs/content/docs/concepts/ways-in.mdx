---
title: Ways in
description: One call, your own shell, or plain TanStack, and how to move between them.
---

formdef adds to TanStack Form and never takes anything away. There are three ways in, named by what
you own. Moving from one to the next keeps the config you already wrote.

| Way | You write | formdef renders | Use it when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **One call** | `createForm(config)` and `<Form options />` | the `<form>` element, the submission and the fields | a form is fields and a submit button; values may come from hooks through `options` |
| **Own shell** | `formHook.useAppForm(…)`, `<form.AppForm>`, the `<form>` element and anything around it | `<Fields form fields layout />`, as often as you like, each with a part of the fields | the shell reacts to form state, a wizard shows one step at a time, your own markup sits between groups, the form posts to a server action |
| **Plain TanStack** | everything | nothing | a form formdef does not help with |

## Moving between them

- `fields` and `layout` have the same types on the first two ways: moving a config to your own
  shell is a copy.
- `formHook` is the very hook `createForm` uses, so both ways share the contexts and the
  registered form components.
- `Fields` also renders into a field group from `withFieldGroup`, so one config renders at
  `billing` and at `shipping`.
- Inside any config, a single field can take a `render` function instead of a widget. It receives
  TanStack's real field.

## Where one call ends

- The submit event is not reachable. Two submit buttons that mean different things pass
  `onSubmitMeta` and call `form.handleSubmit(meta)` from an inline entry.
- An inline entry sees the form's values, its state and the operations on them, but not the
  members whose types depend on the validators, such as `validate` and the error-map setters. A
  form that needs them takes its own shell.
- The `ref` of a generated form is its `<form>` element. The form object itself is reachable
  through `options.listeners.onMount`.
